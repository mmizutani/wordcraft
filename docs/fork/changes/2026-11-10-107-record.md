# A case can keep a record of its negotiation, off until the person turns it on

- **Issue:** #107, AS8.9
- **Upstream doc it would have touched:** `ROADMAP.md`
- **Not verified:** the Record section on a real screen; the tests drive the window headlessly. Windows: whether a rename is refused while another window's record holds a file open is reported as Busy and tested nowhere here. No real-model run: nothing in a turn reads the record.

Until now nothing of a conversation outlived it but the notes a model wrote into the case. Anthropic's components of agent memory name the transcript as the layer the others are consolidated from, and the design's review pass ("Review this case", the next change) needs one. This change is that layer, as an opt-in record per case.

**The data.** `crates/assist/src/cases/log.rs`. A `Record` is its own serde type, tagged `t`, never the pane's `Entry`, since an entry holds internal markers and is rewritten in place: `turn` (when, which turn, what was asked, the answer with markers taken out, each tool call's title, status and detail, the model, the stop reason, the ids recalled and the ids marked used), `decided` (the person's Accept or Reject of anyone's change: decision, kind, author, text), `edited` (what the assistant's text said, what it says now), `redlines` (the other side's tracked changes and comments, each with author, date, text and position as the engine writes one, and how many more there were) and `dropped { n }`. Every text is a `Quoted`, cut on write (an answer at 64 Ki characters, anything else at 2 Ki) so a line is bounded whatever a turn produced. A turn's record is built by the transcript when `Event::Done` arrives, from the pieces, which never held the markers, and the window takes it once with `Assistant::take_finished_turn`; nothing streams.

**The writer.** `CaseLog::open(shelf, at, stamp)` makes `log/` in the case's folder (0700), refuses a link where the folder should be, removes the oldest conversations' files while the record is past 64 MiB until a whole new 16 MiB file has room, opens `log/<stamp>-<n>.jsonl` with `create_new` (0600; a link there fails as a file would) and starts one thread that writes a line a record and stops at 16 MiB, counting the rest as one `dropped` line at the end. No line passes the reader's 1 MiB cap: a `redlines` snapshot that would (every text within its own cap, 64 comments of 2 Ki four-byte characters together) loses its last comments, then its last changes, into `more` until it fits, and any other record that would is counted as dropped. `write` is `try_send` on a bounded channel of 256: a full queue counts the record and the count goes as a `dropped` line in front of the next record that fits. Nothing locks: no two writers share a file. Nothing fsyncs: a power cut can lose the last lines, which a record the review pass only reads can take. Dropping the log on the frame never waits, and a count the full queue can't take then reaches the writer through a shared counter, so its last `dropped` line holds it; `close` joins the thread, and only a worker calls it. `search_log` lists the plain `.jsonl` files (a link inside `log/` is left out, and the file is opened with `O_NOFOLLOW` on Unix), reads each line through a 1 MiB cap, skips and counts a line it can't parse, a line that is too long, or a last line a crash cut, and matches the folded query against the texts people wrote, not field names. `log_size` sums the files; `delete_log` renames `log/` into the shelf's `.trash-` and removes it, so a crash between the two is finished by the next listing.

**Opt-in.** `Case` gained `record: bool`, serialized with a default of off, and `PersonOp::Record(bool)` is the only thing that sets it: no marker, no `ModelWrite`. Two person-only commands under the existing prefix, `assist.memory.case.record {"value": bool}` and `assist.memory.case.recordDelete {}`, hand the window `memory.case.record` and `memory.case.recordDelete`, so `person_only`, the macro guard and the control channel's refuse them with no guard edit; the registry test counts 20 now and `hostile_params_never_panic` covers them. Details › Case › Record is the checkbox **Keep a record of this negotiation** with a note saying what is stored and where, the record's size (a listing of `log/` at most every two seconds), a search box whose search runs on a worker and lists each hit as one line on the page of the case it searched and no other, and **Delete the record**.

**In the window.** `crates/ui-egui/src/assist/cases/record.rs`. `Conversation` gained `log: Option<CaseLog>`, opened when the conversation starts on a case whose record is on, or when the person turns the record on while the conversation runs; the first line is the `redlines` snapshot, everyone's tracked changes and comments but the person's and this assistant's, from two new engine walks (`review::redlines`, `review::comments_except`, with `review::authors` naming the document's revision authors), written only while the window shows the conversation's own document: a log opened again after a delete, with another document on screen, holds no snapshot. Past the walk's cap a run joins nothing of an earlier author's entry, so no text takes another revision's author and date. Before each line the conversation reads its case again and lets the log go when the record was turned off in another window or process. Every frame the finished turn, if any, goes to the conversation's own log: the case the conversation started on, as a marker is routed, never the document's current binding, so a conversation on case A writes A's record after the document is rebound to B. `review_started` now gathers anyone's changes by author as well as the conversation's, only while the conversation keeps a record, and `review_ended` writes a `decided` line per change of each author that went, through the same `resolved` comparison the feedback digest uses, skipping an author whose changes can't be listed before or after (more than the window compares: unknown, not none) and not waiting on the assistant's own list, which unknown skips only the digest's feedback; a script's or the control channel's Accept never reaches it, since only the person's command path calls those hooks. `edits.rs` writes an `edited` line where it records an edit for the digest.

**Deletes.** `memory.case.delete` and `memory.case.recordDelete` run on a worker: the conversation's log is taken out and closed there first, so its file is closed before the folder is renamed, then `Shelf::delete_case` (unchanged, the lessons step still under the case lock) or `delete_log` runs; the result arrives at the next frame, `Busy` or an I/O error as a notice, and nothing is lost. A second delete while one runs is refused. After the delete ends, succeeded or failed, the live conversation on that case opens a new file if its record is still on, since its file was closed for the delete either way. The case delete ends the conversation and drops the binding on the frame, as before.

**Promises.** Every place that said nothing of a conversation is stored now says what the record keeps, that it is off by default, where it lives and how to delete it: `docs/assistant.md` (the feedback paragraph and a new **The record** paragraph in Cases), `crates/assist/src/lib.rs`, `crates/assist/src/feedback.rs` and the window's `assist.rs` header. `rg "Nothing of it is stored" docs/assistant.md crates/assist/src/lib.rs` finds nothing.

**Security review fix.** The first commit followed symlinks: `read_dir` metadata through a link, `File::open` on a link, and `log/` itself could be a link into another client's case, which `store.rs` guards against everywhere else. Now the folder must be a real directory, `files_in` keeps only entries whose `file_type` is a plain file, reads open with `O_NOFOLLOW`, and `make_room` removes only what `files_in` listed. The test `a_link_in_or_as_the_record_folder_is_never_followed` (Unix) points `log/` and a `.jsonl` inside it at another client's record and checks nothing is read, written or removed through either; it failed at its first assertion before the fix.

**Known limits.** A partial Accept or Reject of a change (a selection inside one insertion) isn't recorded: `resolved` keeps feedback conservative and says nothing of it. With several conversations open on one case near the 64 MiB cap, opening another can prune a file another window is still writing, and concurrent files can pass the cap. A process running as the person that swaps `log/` for a link between the check and the open isn't defended against: it can already read every case directly. Opening a conversation's log, the record switch, the record's recheck before a line and the size line do small file work on the frame, as the case page's other writes do; search and deletes run on workers.

**Deviations from the design.** The search is a page read on a worker with no command, since no command reads a case; the design's command list named `.log`. The redlines snapshot is written when the conversation's log opens (its first ask with the record on, or the record turned on mid-conversation) rather than at bind, since binding ends the conversation and the next ask opens a log anyway. The record's on/off switch lives on `Case` as a field through a `PersonOp`, as the brief suggested.

**Tests.** `cases::log::tests`: one record is one line and search finds it; a truncated last line is skipped and counted; a full queue never blocks and becomes a `dropped` record (a gated writer, two in the queue); the caps hold and the oldest conversation goes first; delete removes the record and twice is nothing; hostile lines are skipped and counted (the over-long line a whole `turn`, so only the length guard refuses it); a log dropped with its queue full still ends the file with the count; a snapshot over the line cap is shrunk into `more` and read back; the link test above. `cmd::review::tests`: past the cap a redline never extends an earlier author's entry. `assist::cases::record::tests`: off by default no file is written; on, a finished turn is one line with its markers taken out and nothing streams (a held turn, cancelled); rejecting or accepting the other side's insertion is a `decided` record naming the author, and a script's Reject is none; a conversation on A writes only A's record after the document is rebound to B; a control-channel click or a macro step on the record's commands does nothing; Delete the record removes it (the old text no longer found, only the reopened file's snapshot left) and deleting the case runs off the frame and leaves no folder; a record opened again after a delete holds nothing of another document shown meanwhile; a search of one case shows nothing on another; the record turned off through the shelf, as another window would, stops the conversation writing at its next turn; the assistant's changes past the cap still leave the other side's decision recorded; a failed delete (the case folder unwritable) leaves the conversation recording. The engine's registry and shape tests cover the two commands, and the scripted-writes window test gained them.
