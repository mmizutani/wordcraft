# A question about one clause gets the clause ladder: every position on it, this case's and the client's, in one item

- **Issue:** #107, AS8.8
- **Upstream doc it would have touched:** `ROADMAP.md`
- **Not verified:** the rungs on a real screen beyond one `ui_shot`; the test drives the pane headlessly. The rung order is a guess at what the question wants, with no real-model run behind it.

"What did we concede on the liability cap, and when were we weaker?" was answered until now by whichever positions ranked, three per other case, under the budget: a concession won while stronger, aged and read from a case with no facts, scored under `MIN_SCORE` and was left out. Now a topic of the query with at least three `Position` notes in the pool goes as one ladder item in their place.

**The ladder.** `ladder(topic, pool, now)` in `recall.rs` is a pure read: this case's positions on the topic, confirmed or not, and the same client's confirmed ones (the pool already holds nothing else of a neighbor, so a model's note never leaves its case), each as a `Rung` with its place and title, date, who moved, the terms, what was traded, whether it landed, the balance then (`stance_on`) against now, and its standing. Lessons, patterns and pitfalls never enter. It is computed per turn and stored nowhere, as the design argued: nothing to sync, nothing to go stale, and deleting a case removes its rungs with no other write. The order is the rung whose balance is closest to now first (an unknown balance last), then landed before open, then newest, because the precedent that transfers is the one taken under the same balance, and a landed position is a fact where an open one is an ask. At most `MAX_RUNGS` (8) go; the rest are counted in `left_out`.

**In `compose`.** The ladder takes the rank of its best rung: the first laddered note the ranking reaches pushes the ladder, and every later note on that topic is skipped without being counted, since it is in the ladder. It counts as one item against `MAX_ITEMS` and its JSON against `BUDGET`; its rungs count toward no neighbor's cap and bring no balance item, since each rung carries its own balance. If its best rung is under `MIN_SCORE` or it doesn't fit, its rungs are counted as left out. Under three positions nothing changes, and the corpus tests are untouched.

**The item.** `ItemId::Ladder { topic }` shows as `ladder:liability`, disjoint from `n12`, `<case>:n12`, `<case>:balance`, `l3` and `m1`, so `[[used: ladder:liability]]` makes a chip as the others do. Its JSON is `{"id":"ladder:liability","kind":"ladder","topic":"liability","text":"liability: 3 positions, 2 landed","place":"this case and the same client's cases","rungs":[{"place":…,"on":…,"moved":…,"terms":…,"tradedFor":…,"landed":…,"balance":"then …, now …","confirmed":…}],"balance":"then unknown, now …","confirmed":<every rung confirmed>}`, the rung fields named as a position note's are. `RecalledItem` carries the rungs (`rungs`, empty for every other item), so the pane and the quote index read what was sent: every rung's terms and trade are in `sent_text`, and a pasted rung is flagged like a pasted note. `Told` keys a ladder by its rungs' content, not its id alone, so a retaining session told a ladder is told it again once a position was added.

**The pane.** Details › Recalled shows a ladder as its id and line, then each rung on three wrapped lines, since the pane is too narrow for a table: date, where (with "unconfirmed" on this case's unconfirmed rung), who moved, terms, traded for, landed or open, and the balance then and now. No file is read on the frame.

**Tests.** `cases::recall::tests`, selected by `ladder`: it derives from `Position` notes alone and orders by balance, landed, date; under three positions the notes go as before and from three one ladder replaces them with every rung's balance gap; through the shelf, deleting a neighbor removes its rungs on the next load with no other write; a neighbor's unconfirmed position is no rung and its canary is nowhere in the sent JSON; a rung's terms and trade are flagged by the quote check; a ladder whose rungs changed is new to a session that was told it. `assist::cases::tests::details_shows_a_ladders_rungs_inside_the_pane` drives the window with three positions, reads every rung, and checks each rung's terms and trade end inside the window; a seven-column table first ran past its right edge.
