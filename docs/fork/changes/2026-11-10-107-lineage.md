# The Assistant pane recognises a later draft of a case's contract and offers one click to bind it

- **Issue:** #107, AS8.11
- **Upstream doc it would have touched:** `ROADMAP.md`
- **Not verified:** the card on a real screen; the tests drive the window headlessly. The threshold and the trivial-paragraph floor on real contracts rather than the synthetic ones in the tests.

Cases are bound to documents by the person, always, and a binding lasts while the document is open in one window: after a restart or a reopen the person binds again. This change is the one click that replaces finding the case in the list.

**The print.** `cases::lineage::DraftPrint` is a paragraph-text fingerprint of one document: each body paragraph's text, folded as the case text is (`text::folded`: case, full-width ASCII and whitespace), hashed with SHA-256 and cut to its first four bytes, and the 256 smallest distinct hashes kept (a `BTreeSet` that pops its largest past 256, so the work is bounded for a document of any size). A paragraph with fewer than 24 letters and digits is left out: an empty line, a heading number, "(a)" or a signature label is in every contract and says nothing about lineage. At most 4 KiB of one paragraph is read before hashing. The print carries the day and the document's title as the window shows it, and no text. Eight full prints add about 23 KiB to `case.json`; the full-case test now holds eight and still fits 1 MiB.

**Overlap** is containment of the new document in the old draft, not Jaccard: a later draft adds and drops clauses, and what matters is how much of what is on screen was there before. While neither print was cut at 256 the result is exact (the common hashes over the new document's). A cut print holds every hash of its document up to its largest one, so past that the two are compared over the hashes at or below the smallest such cutoff: the share of the new document's hashes in that range that the old draft has too. The hashes are spread evenly, so the range samples the paragraphs evenly. A test cuts both prints and checks the estimate within a few points.

**Storing.** `Case.drafts`, serde default empty, oldest first, at most eight (the oldest goes); a file holding more, or hashes out of order, is clamped and reads as damaged, as every other over-limit field does. Only `PersonOp::AddDraft` adds one, so a model's marker can't: `ModelWrite` has no such variant. The window adds it when the person binds (`memory.case.bind` and `memory.case.new`), on a worker thread from a clone of the document, through `Shelf::person` with the click the guard already saw. Nothing of a print reaches a turn: `compose` reads notes, balance, facts and title, `compose_review` names its fields, and `no_turn_carries_a_print` binds a canary document to a case and checks that neither recall's sources nor the review's carry the paragraph text, the hash number, or the words `drafts` or `hashes`.

**The suggestion.** `assist::cases::suggest` in the window. While the pane shows a document bound to no case, `refresh` starts a worker (the listing worker's pattern: a thread, a channel, a repaint) that takes the document's print from a clone and compares it with the prints of the 128 most recently changed cases across every wall, reading at most 16 MiB of their files (`Shelf::newest_cases`), as the shelf reads them (a link refused, a damaged file skipped but counted against both bounds). The best match at or above **half** becomes a card at the top of the pane, before the tabs: the percentage, the case's title and its client, and **Use it** (runs the person-only `assist.memory.case.bind`), **Not this** (a set of document ids in this window; no file write) and **New case** (Details, where the form is). Half, because a later draft keeps most clauses while two contracts from one template share the boilerplate and differ in the rest: below half the card would show on every document made from a common template. The percentage is shown either way, so the person decides the ambiguous middle. A document with fewer than eight printed paragraphs is compared with nothing, since a near-empty document matches any case on its signature block. The print is taken once per document shown, and again after 64 accepted changes while it stays unbound, or at once when a document whose print was too short to compare (blank, or only numbering and headings) gains or loses a paragraph (the PR review bot found the first version counted raw paragraphs, so ten numbering lines read as long), so a contract pasted into a blank document gets the card as it lands (one change, which the 64-change rule alone missed: the screenshot for this PR showed no card until that was fixed, and `a_draft_pasted_into_a_blank_document_is_suggested` checks it), while typing in a long document doesn't hash it every frame. The result goes to the pane and nowhere else: not to a model, a log or a file.

**Deviations from the design.** The design's pane header showed **No case** or the case's title; the pane has no such header today (Details › Case does that), so the card alone is added, above the tabs, and only while there is a suggestion. The design named `DraftPrint::of(doc, name, on)` and `overlap`; `overlap` is the containment estimate rather than Jaccard, for the reason above, and the doc comment says which. The design's card said "Acme MSA"; the pane also names the client, since two clients can have a case of one title.

**Tests.** `cargo test -p wordcraft-assist cases::lineage`: a later draft (five clauses edited, four added, three removed) overlaps its first draft at 22/31 and an unrelated contract at zero; two contracts from one twelve-clause template with eight own clauses each overlap at 60%; folding and whitespace don't change a hash and a changed word does; trivial paragraphs aren't printed; a print of a document of 3,001 paragraphs holds 256 sorted distinct hashes and, serialized, none of the paragraph text; a cut print still estimates its overlap with a whole one; a case keeps the newest eight prints, reads a file without the field, and clamps a file with nine or with unsorted hashes as damaged; no turn carries a print. `cargo test -p wordcraft-ui-egui assist::cases::suggest`: a later draft of a bound case's document is suggested with that case, 71% and the client's name, an unrelated one isn't, and a suggestion writes nothing; two contracts from one template are suggested at 60%, never bound without a click, Not this hides it for that document without a file write, and another document in the window is suggested again; a control-channel click on Use it binds nothing and the person's click binds and adds the second print. Each test failed before its code (the print never reached the case; the stub returned nothing) and each was mutation-checked on a detached copy.

**After review.** Overlap first sampled the k smallest hashes of the union, which could miss a complete match: a whole eight-paragraph extract whose hashes sat at positions 100 to 107 of a cut print scored 0% rather than 100%, and now scores 100%, and a cut print against a larger whole one equals a brute-force count over the range the cut print holds in full. A paragraph repeated once the print was full still evicted its largest hash, so a print could hold 255 hashes or depend on paragraph order; the largest now goes only when the hash is new, and the same paragraphs with repeats first or last give the bottom 256 of the distinct set. The scan listed every case (reading each file) and then opened each again with no bound, and a document shown while a worker ran started another beside it; now `Shelf::newest_cases` stats every case file, sorts them by when they last changed, since the listing is sorted by id and holds no prints, and reads each of the newest once, stopping at 128 files or 16 MiB; a new document waits until the running worker returns, whose result is dropped when it is for another document. A test holds a worker while three documents are shown and counts one started, and another puts the matching case behind 130 newer ones and gets no card until it changes last. The work handed to a thread was lost when no thread could be made, as on the web, so neither the suggestion nor a bound document's print happened there; it now waits in a slot the thread takes it from and runs on the frame when the spawn fails, which a test that refuses threads shows for both.

**Known limits.** A control-channel `ui.screenshot` captures the card, with another client's case title and the percentage; Details › Case has listed every client's cases for an unbound document since AS8.4, which a screenshot captures too, so hiding the card alone would be a false boundary, and whether control screenshots should mask the case pages is a separate decision. The card's sentence can be selected and copied, which is the person's own act on their own screen, as with every case title on that page, and nothing reaches a model. A matching case changed less recently than the 128 newest gets no card.

**From the PR review bot.** Binding a damaged case wrote its print through the same path as an edit, which moves a damaged file aside and keeps only what could be read: binding alone quietly repaired a truncated case and made it recallable. A print now leaves a damaged case as it is (`Shelf::person` refuses `AddDraft` on one), and only an edit the person makes repairs it; `a_draft_print_never_repairs_a_damaged_case` checks the file is untouched and nothing is moved aside.
