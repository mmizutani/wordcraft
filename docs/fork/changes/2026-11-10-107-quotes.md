# A change that quotes what the assistant recalled from past cases says so before you accept it

- **Issue:** #107, AS8.5
- **Upstream doc it would have touched:** `ROADMAP.md`
- **Not verified:** the pane on a real screen; the tests drive it headlessly

A document can talk a model into pasting a past case's fallback position into a tracked change or a comment, and the file may go to the other side. Nothing in the data channel can prevent that, so each output row, when the transcript first sees it and again when its words grow, is checked against what the conversation recalled. A shared run of 36 bytes sets `OutputRow.quotes`, and Details › Output shows "This quotes your case notes" in the warning colour on the row, beside Show, Accept and Reject while it has them and on its own once it is accepted, rejected or resolved: an accepted insertion's words are in the document. The comparison folds case and full-width ASCII with the ranker's own `fold`, so the two never disagree on what is the same text.

What the row is checked against is every text the items carried to the model, field by field (terms, what was traded for, traps, guards, how, a balance's factors), not only the line the pane shows for each. The transcript keeps a hash of every 36-byte window of that text, apart from its entries, so trimming an old turn out of the conversation doesn't forget what the model was given. The set holds at most 393,216 windows (64 turns of the 6 KiB recall budget, in ASCII, which has a window at every byte; about 12 MiB when full); past that, the oldest recall's windows go first and the dropped count is kept. Checking a row is one hash lookup per window of the row's own text, whatever was recalled, and the row's text is its whole change or comment as far as 8 KiB, not the 120 characters the pane shows.

It is a flag and not a wall. A paraphrase, a translation, or a run split by a different line break passes, as does a paste past the 8 KiB kept of one change. It looks at what was recalled in this conversation, including earlier turns, and at nothing a person typed.
